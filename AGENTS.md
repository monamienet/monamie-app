# AGENTS.md

## Project Overview

This application is maintained by the company MonAmieNet and provides clients (organizations) services for tracking, coordination and comunication of teams.

## Business Architecture

MonAmie publishes a **single app** on its Google Developer Account: `org.monamienet.app`, named "MonAmie" (launcher, Play listing and native splash are the same for everyone). Each organization (client) is on one of two **plans** (`plan` in the central registry `organizations/{id}`):
- **Standard**: its own Firebase project `monamienet-<id>`, on the organization's own billing account;
- **Community** (free): the shared project `monamienet-community`, owned and paid by MonAmieNet, restricted to the *Economia* tracking preset (one position every 15 minutes at most, enforced by the rules through `organizations/{id}/config/plan.minIntervalMinutes`). The central project and the Community project stay separate projects (Auth, IAM and quotas are per project).

On both plans **every organization's data lives under `organizations/{organizationId}/…`** of its project: `config/branding` (publicly readable), `config/plan`, `config/tracking` (the administrators' period and radius, followed live by the background service; the map's one freshness threshold, the validity, is fixed at 12 minutes, see `docs/pt-br/compartilhamento.md`), `organizationMemberships/{personKey}` and `groups/{groupId}` with `groupMemberships/{personKey}` (who belongs, see below), and `groups/{groupId}/positions/{personKey}` (a person's last position in that group: `email`, `name`, `lat`, `lng`, `capturedAt` on the device clock, `receivedAt` on the server clock, `isPublishing`, `removedAt`, null while they publish there) with `trail/{pointId}` and `trailDays/{yyyy-mm-dd}` (a per-day summary, `lastAt` and `pointCount`, written in the same batch as each position, so the history calendar reads at most one document per person and day). A Standard project is simply a project holding one organization, so code, rules and tests are the same for both plans; no path is ever read at the root. The rules scope every match by organization (the caller's membership documents) and test cross-organization access. Collection and field names live in one place per codebase: `FirestorePaths` (`lib/data/services/firebase/firestore_paths.dart`, also used by `tool/`) and `firestore_paths.js` in `functions/` and `central/functions/`; use them instead of string literals.

The app binds the organization **at runtime**, after Google sign-in, never at build time:
1. Google sign-in goes through the central MonAmie project `monamienet-app` (the `[DEFAULT]` Firebase app). Only one OAuth client may exist per package + SHA-1, so it lives there, and every organization project allows the central web client under "Allowed client IDs".
2. The central `resolveOrganizations` function (`central/functions/`) reads the user's organizations from `accounts/{personKey}` in the central project (an index the central functions keep next to the memberships, see below), keeping those whose entry in the central registry `organizations/{id}` has a `status` the app shows. The status follows a fixed lifecycle (`reserved` → `awaiting_billing`/`provisioning` → `active`, plus `failed`, `migrating` and `deleting`) written only by the central functions; its timed exits delete the organization's project and registry entry and retire the id forever in `retiredOrganizationIds/{id}` (see `docs/en-us/white-label.md`, *Organization status*). Organizations are created in the app (`createOrganization`; the creator becomes the owner and first administrator) and an account is deleted in the app with 30 days to cancel (`accountDeletions/{personKey}`; an owner's organization goes to `deleting` with it); see the guide's sections 11 and 12.
3. The app binds the chosen organization (`OrganizationSession`): its Firebase project (a named app `organization-<id>`) and its branding, together, and remembers the binding on the device (`SharedPreferences`) until sign-out or removal from the organization (its `organizationMemberships/{personKey}` disappears).

Where each value lives:
- **Organization's choice**: name (required) and a catalog `themeId` in `organizations/<id>/config/branding`, plus an optional logo, one square 512 px PNG at `organizations/<id>/logo.png` in the organization's Cloud Storage (converted on an administrator's device from any format; never an SVG). Administrators change them in the app (*Aparência*, through the `setBranding` function, which validates the theme against the central catalog); `dart run tool/organization.dart publish <id> --name … --theme …` sets name and theme for organizations onboarded by hand.
- **MonAmie's choice**: the organization's `status` in `organizations/{id}` and the **theme catalog** `themes/{id}` (`name`, `primary`, `dark`, `light`, `onPrimary`, `onDark`, `order`, `active`), both in the central project. Organizations pick a theme, never free colors; an unknown or missing `themeId` falls back to Grafite, and a retired theme (`active: false`) still resolves. The catalog is seeded with `dart run tool/organization.dart themes` and is publicly readable.
- **Derived by rule**, never stored: project `monamienet-<id>` (Standard) or `monamienet-community` (Community, from `plan`), and `personKey(email)`, the key of a person's documents.
- **Names never side by side**: "MonAmie" appears alone on MonAmie's own screens (launcher, Play listing, splash, sign-in, organization picker, organization creation); once an organization is bound, the app shows only the organization's name. `publish` refuses an organization name starting with "MonAmie ".
- **Owned by another system**, fetched, never versioned: the central `google-services.json` (build time, `tool/organization.dart firebase`) and each organization's Firebase options (runtime, `resolveOrganizations`).

Rules that come from the plugins' source code: never `delete()` an organization `FirebaseApp` (cloud_firestore caches instances per app name forever); the organization app is created natively in `MonAmieApplication.kt` so the UI and the tracking service engines never race to create it; the binding lives in `SharedPreferences`, not Hive, because two isolates read it.

**Resuming sharing after a restart** (Android only): the map menu's *Retomar ao reiniciar* switch (off by default, the device's choice, kept across sign-outs) makes sharing come back by itself after the device restarts or the app is updated, only if the person was sharing then. Everything lives in the classic SharedPreferences file `monamie_sharing_resume` (`SharingResumePreferencesService`, mirrored in `ResumeFailures.kt`; classic, not DataStore, because Kotlin reads it and two isolates write it): the option, the intent (`{email, name}`, written when sharing starts and removed when it stops and in `SignOut`), the texts the device shows without the screen (stored from the ARB files by `SharingNotificationRefresher`), a `resuming` mark and a `failure` reason. `ResumeSharingReceiver.kt` (`BOOT_COMPLETED`, `MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED`; the plugin's `autoStartOnBoot` stays off) decides with `ResumeDecision` before starting anything: without location all the time or with the battery "Restricted" it only notifies. The service's `onStart` publishes as the stored intent without waiting for the screen, waits for the GPS when it is off, and when the account or the publisher groups are gone writes the `failure` key, which a listener `MonAmieApplication` installs turns into a notification. A resume that fails, in the receiver or in the service, forgets the intent: the screen then shows sharing off, and nothing comes back until the person shares again.

Onboarding an organization needs no app release: see `docs/en-us/white-label.md`. CI only ships the app: Cloud Functions and rules are deployed by hand, `central/` once to `monamienet-app` and `functions/` + `firestore.rules` + `firestore.indexes.json` + `storage.rules` to every organization project.

### Organizations, Groups and Users

Who belongs where lives in Firestore documents of the organization's project, **written only by the central functions** (`central/functions/`, Admin SDK) and by `dart run tool/organization.dart add-admin`; the rules let no client write them.

- **personKey**: every document about a person is keyed by `personKey(email)`, the lowercase-hex SHA-256 of the trimmed, lowercased e-mail, so no e-mail appears in a document name; each document also stores `email`. Twins that must agree: `person_key.js` in `central/functions/` and `functions/`, `lib/data/services/firebase/person_key.dart` (also used by `tool/`), and `personKey()` in `firestore.rules` and `storage.rules`. Fixed test vector: `ana@example.com` → `8e43ca37…a018e11`.
- **Organization membership** `organizations/{id}/organizationMemberships/{personKey}`: `{email, role: 'admin' | 'member', addedAt}`. Having one is being in the organization; it disappearing is being removed.
- **Groups** `organizations/{id}/groups/{groupId}` (`{name, createdAt}`, Firestore auto ids, never reused) and **group memberships** `groups/{groupId}/groupMemberships/{personKey}`: `{email, organizationId, roles, addedAt}`, roles sorted and non-empty among `publisher` (publishes their own position in that group), `subscriber` (sees the group's map, trail and calendar) and `maintainer` (the *Gestor do grupo*, see below; sees the map only if also `subscriber`). A person finds their groups with a collection-group query on `groupMemberships` by `email` and `organizationId` (indexes in `firestore.indexes.json`).
- **Administrators** (*administradores*) are the people whose organization membership has `role: 'admin'`, and **only** they: administration powers (groups, invites, appearance, tracking settings, seeing every group) come from `organizations/{id}/organizationMemberships`, written only by the central functions. The rules, `storage.rules` (the logo), `setBranding` and the central functions all read that same document.
- **Group maintainers** (*Gestor do grupo*, the `maintainer` role) manage **only their own group**, through the same central functions (`group_access.js`): its people screen (`listGroups` returns them only the groups they maintain), invites to it (`publisher`/`subscriber` only, never `firstGroupName`), listing, resending and cancelling its invites, changing people's `publisher`/`subscriber` roles (`setMemberRoles` keeps the target's `maintainer` state as it is), removing people (never another maintainer) and renaming it. They never create or delete groups, give or take `maintainer` (administrators do) or touch the organization's settings. The rules let them read their group's `groupMemberships`; nothing else changes in what they read.
- **The owner** (*titular* in the app, a gender-neutral word) is the person who created the organization, recorded as `ownerEmail` in the central registry `organizations/{id}`. The owner is always an administrator: creation writes their `admin` membership, and no function demotes or removes them. Only the owner chooses the other administrators, on *Administradores* (`listAdministrators`, `setAdministrator`): an existing member becomes `admin`, an administrator goes back to `member`; a demoted administrator in no group stays in the organization as a member in no group (never removed silently). Deleting their account deletes the organization (confirmed by typing its id), and `resolveOrganizations` tells the app so (`owner`). A non-owner who deletes their account leaves every group and the organization.
- **`accounts/{personKey}`** in the central project, `{email, organizationIds}`: an index of a person's organizations for `resolveOrganizations`, written by the central functions *after* the organization project ("the organization wins": an extra id is dropped by the app when it finds no membership; a missing one is reported by `dart run tool/organization.dart check`). No client reads it. Retiring an organization removes its id from every entry.
- **Changing roles** = `setMemberRoles` (an administrator gives exactly the roles sent, `maintainer` included; a maintainer only `publisher`/`subscriber`); losing `publisher` (or leaving the group with `removeMember`) marks the person's position in that group with `removedAt`, so the map leaves it out and the history keeps it. Leaving one's last group also leaves the organization, unless one is an administrator. Joining happens only through an invite (`acceptInvite`: organization membership `member` if absent plus the group membership, one batch).
- **In the app**: what a person may do (`Capabilities`) comes from listening to their own organization membership and, with the collection-group query, to their group memberships (`MembershipRepository`, implemented by `MembershipRepositoryFirestore` over `MembershipService`), so a change made by an administrator shows at once and a missing organization membership makes the app forget the organization (`SignOut.afterAccessLost`, then *Sem organização* saying why). The same repository holds the group on the map, which listens to `groups/{groupId}/positions` with `removedAt == null` (`PositionsRepository`); the history reads every position of the group (removed people included), its `trail` and its `trailDays`. There is no "active group": while sharing is on, the background service (its own isolate, its own listener on the person's memberships) publishes each position in **every** group where the person is `publisher`, one batch per group (position, trail point, day summary), and a group that refuses does not stop the others. The administrators' and maintainers' screens call the central group and invite functions with `groupId`; the menu shows *Grupos* to administrators, *Meus grupos* to maintainers and *Administradores* to the owner only.
- **Who runs it**: the membership-writing calls run as `groups-admin@monamienet-app.iam.gserviceaccount.com` (it keeps its old name; it needs `roles/datastore.user` on the central project, on the Community project and on each Standard project); the scheduled expiry and erase run as `partner-expirer@`.


## Frontend & Architectural Best Practices

### Folders
- **Layers as in the official Flutter architecture guide** (the Compass app): `lib/ui/<feature>/view_models/` (`*_view_model.dart`, `*ViewModel`) and `lib/ui/<feature>/widgets/` (screens are `*_screen.dart`, `*Screen`); widgets and themes shared by every feature in `lib/ui/core/ui/` and `lib/ui/core/themes/`; `lib/data/repositories/` and `lib/data/services/` (Firebase setup in `lib/data/services/firebase/`, Firestore services in `lib/data/services/firestore/`, Cloud Functions in `lib/data/services/functions/`, the Hive model and `OrganizationBinding` in `lib/data/model/`); domain models in `lib/domain/models/` and use cases in `lib/domain/use_cases/`; routes in `lib/routing/`; the composition root in `lib/config/`; `Result` and other helpers in `lib/utils/`. `test/` mirrors `lib/` and `tool/`.

### View Models & State
- **View models are `ChangeNotifier`s** (`package:flutter/foundation.dart`) holding one immutable state class made with `freezed` (`<name>_state.dart` next to the view model, its generated `.freezed.dart` committed). They replace the state whole with `copyWith` and call `notifyListeners()` only when the new state differs (`==`); lists in a state are never changed in place. Models a state holds have value equality (`freezed`), so the deep comparison works.
- **Shared state lives in repositories, never in a view model**: what several view models and screens of the bound organization read is held by organization-scoped repositories (`MembershipRepository`, `ObservedDayRepository`, `PermissionsRepository`, `TrackingSettingsRepository.settings`), and a view model's constructor takes repositories and use cases, never another view model.
- **State contracts other classes react to value by value** (`MembershipRepository`, `ObservedDayRepository`, `PermissionsRepository`, `CalendarViewModel`, `TrackingViewModel`) expose one `ValueListenable` per value instead, each notifying only when its content changes, so a listener reacts to the one value it needs; widgets that read several combine them with `Listenable.merge`.
- **Dependencies through the constructor**, results as `Result`: view models never look up services, show messages or navigate; screens show the messages. A failure is data, never text: `Err(failure, {error, stackTrace})` carries a `Failure` (`lib/utils/failure.dart`, a freezed `sealed` class with one case per distinct thing that can go wrong and the values its text needs, e.g. `Failure.connection(FailedAction.save)`, `Failure.groupNameTaken(name)`, `Failure.serverRefusal(message)`), and a `UserNotice` (`lib/utils/user_notice.dart`) is a `sealed` kind too (`UserNotice.waitingForGps()`, `UserNotice.failure(failure)`…). Repositories, use cases, domain validators and view models return them; states keep a `Failure?` (`null` when nothing failed) instead of an error string; only the UI turns them into words, with `FailureText`/`UserNoticeText` (`lib/ui/core/ui/failure_text.dart`) over `AppLocalizations`.
- **Screens listen with `ListenableBuilder`** and receive their view model as a parameter; they run the view model's `load` command when they open and keep their `TextEditingController`s themselves (view models import no Flutter UI library, checked by `dart run tool/check_layers.dart`).
- **Views are dumb**: a screen or widget holds no logic beyond simple conditionals, animation, layout and routing. Flow state (a wizard's step, a ticked checkbox, "just deleted"), decisions (who shows on the map, whether a name may be saved) and data shaping live in the view models; widgets keep only their `TextEditingController`s, focus and pure UI state (a tab, a collapsed sheet, a sheet's choice before it is saved). Screens receive view models only, never a repository or service: the map gets `HomeViewModel` (the memberships, the permissions and the Meet call, each value the repository's own `ValueListenable`) and *Grupo* gets the route-owned `GroupInvitesViewModel` next to the shared `GroupsViewModel`. The administration screens share their app bar, buttons, confirmation sheet and toast in `lib/ui/core/ui/admin_ui.dart`.

### Navigation & Injection
- **`go_router` with `MaterialApp.router`** (`lib/routing/router.dart`, paths in `routes.dart`). The sign-in flow never navigates: `SessionRepository` (`lib/data/repositories/session/`, created by the composition root) holds the app-wide `SessionState` (where the flow stands, with what its screen opens with: the picker's choices, the invites, the pending deletion, the invite to welcome), the router listens to it (`refreshListenable`) and the pure `sessionRedirect(session, location)` (`session_redirect.dart`) picks the screen. The use cases `EnterOrganization` (sign-in → pending deletion → directory and branding → binding, organization sign-in and membership check) and `SignOut` (stop tracking first, then sign out, forget the organization; also switching organization, losing access and the account's deletion) move it; each runs one call at a time, app-wide. Each entry screen has its own view model over them: `LoginViewModel` (also the web button's sign-ins), `OrganizationChoiceViewModel` (*Sem organização* and the picker, invites included), `DeletionPendingViewModel`, `WelcomeViewModel`; `HomeSessionViewModel` gives the map's menu its sign-out, switch, owner-only entry and the overlay while leaving (`SessionRepository.leaving`).
- **One composition root**: `lib/config/dependencies.dart` (`appDependencies`, provided in `main` above the app) creates the concrete data layer (Firebase, Hive, SharedPreferences, plugins) under its contracts; view models and screens never create one, and their constructors take every dependency. The background service isolate composes its own.
- **Two `provider` scopes** of view models, read only in route assembly (route builders hand view models to screens as parameters): the app scope (`MonAmieApp`, above the router) builds the router over `SessionRepository`, holds the theme, rebuilt on every change of session (the bound organization's, MonAmie's when none), and follows the `LanguageRepository` for the app's locale; the organization's `ShellRoute` (`OrganizationScope`) creates, starts and disposes the bound organization's shared state (the repositories above and the `StartSharing` use case over them) and then its view models (`CalendarViewModel`, `UserViewModel`, `TrackingViewModel`), so each is disposed before what it listens to. View models used by one screen are created by its route and disposed when it closes.
- **Inside the organization's shell open screens with `push`; leave it only through the session** (a redirect, i.e. a `go`, which disposes the shell). Dialogs and sheets open on the root navigator, outside the scopes: they receive view models as parameters, never through `context.read`.

### Asynchronous Actions & Visual Feedback
- **Immediate Visual Feedback**: Any interactive trigger initiating an asynchronous operation (background services, location hardware, authentication, remote queries) must immediately render a visible pending/loading state (e.g., progress spinner, transitional colors).
- **Every action is a Command**: each action a screen triggers in a view model (`load`, `save`, `create`, `toggleService`, `logout`…) is a `Command0<T>` or `Command1<T, A>` field (`lib/utils/command.dart`, adapted from the Compass app, no package). A command is a `ChangeNotifier` with `running`, `error`, `completed`, `result` and `clearResult()`; its `execute(...)` ignores a call while it runs (or while its `blocked` predicate says so, for the commands of one view model that must not overlap) and returns `null` then, resets `running` in a `finally`, and otherwise returns the action's `Result`. The action returns `Result` and does not throw. State classes keep the data (including the `Failure` shown inline), never an "in flight" flag a command already gives; `Command1.argument` tells which item runs (e.g. the row with the spinner).
- **Screens follow the commands**: they listen to them (`ListenableBuilder`, with `Listenable.merge` next to the view model), disable the trigger (`onPressed: null`) and show the spinner while `running`, and show the `Err` that `execute` returns (`runReportingErrors` for a `SnackBar`, `sheetFailureOf` in an action sheet), its failure named by `FailureText`. Use cases (`lib/domain/use_cases/`) keep their own one-at-a-time guards, since several view models call them.

### Contract & Interface Segregation (SOLID)
- **Dedicated Contract Files**: When creating abstract interfaces/contracts for widgets or view models (e.g., to support dependency injection and testability), place the interface in its own dedicated file rather than inside the concrete implementation file.
- **Avoid Transitive Bloat**: Pure UI components and widget tests must only depend on lightweight contracts, avoiding transitive dependencies on heavy concrete view models that import background services, map rendering, or native hardware plugins.
- **State Never Imports the UI**: View models (`*_view_model.dart`) and state contracts (files with the line `// check_layers: state_contract`) under `lib/` never import Flutter's UI libraries (`material.dart`, `widgets.dart`, `cupertino.dart`, `painting.dart`, `rendering.dart` and the like); `foundation.dart`, `services.dart` and `dart:ui` are allowed. `dart run tool/check_layers.dart` enforces it in the PR checks, together with four more rules: no file under `lib/` imports GetX (`package:get`); no hand-written file under `lib/ui/` or `lib/domain/` imports a plugin (`firebase_*`, `cloud_*`, `geolocator`, `permission_handler`, `flutter_background_service`, `hive*`, `shared_preferences`, `google_sign_in`, `http`), which they reach through a service or repository under `lib/data/`; `lib/domain/` imports nothing from `lib/data/`, `lib/ui/`, `lib/routing/` or `lib/config/`, except its use cases (`lib/domain/use_cases/`), which may import `lib/data/repositories/`, `lib/data/services/` and `lib/data/model/`; and no hand-written file under `lib/ui/` other than a state file holds a hard-coded text (see *Language Conventions*).
- **Services and repositories**: a service (`lib/data/services/`) is a stateless class wrapping one external source (Cloud Functions, Firestore, Storage, Hive, a plugin) that returns `Future`/`Stream` of raw data and may throw; a repository (`lib/data/repositories/<subject>/`, an abstract class plus its implementation) is the source of truth for one kind of data and converts raw data to domain models. The Cloud Functions repositories return `Result` and never throw (`functionsResult` and `functionsRefusal` in `lib/data/repositories/functions_result.dart`: a refusal the server explains becomes a `ServerRefusal` with the function's own words, written in pt-BR by `central/functions/` and shown as they came in every language; anything else a `ConnectionFailure` or `LoadFailure` of the operation); the Firestore ones (`PositionsRepository`, `ThemeRepository`, `TrackingSettingsRepository`) and `MembershipService` throw, since their failures are only logged by the view models; so do `SessionRepository`'s sign-in methods, whose failures the use cases and view models turn into `Failure`s. **Use cases** (`lib/domain/use_cases/`, plain classes returning `Result`) exist only where logic crosses repositories (`EnterOrganization`, `SignOut`, and `StartSharing`: battery restriction, GPS, publishing capability, first fix and the tracking service's start, in that order; organization-scoped). Firestore conversions (`Timestamp`, field names) live in the data layer, next to the code that reads them; domain models import no plugin.

### Hardware & Sensor Operations UX
- **User-Facing Transparency**: Never silently swallow hardware or sensor timeouts (e.g., GPS satellite fix) with developer-only `debugPrint`. When a service starts but sensor calibration is pending, provide transparent, non-blocking user feedback (e.g., an informative SnackBar or status banner) clarifying that the service is running while satellites/hardware sync.
- **Defensive Timeouts**: Guard platform hardware and sensor calls (e.g., `Geolocator.getCurrentPosition()`) with sensible timeouts (e.g., 5 seconds) and `try/catch` blocks to prevent freezing or unhandled crashes during cold starts or weak-signal environments.

### Crash Reporting

- **Firebase Crashlytics, in the central project** `monamienet-app` (the `[DEFAULT]` app), for every organization and plan: one dashboard, no step per organization. Reports carry no person identifier (no e-mail, name or `personKey`) and no coordinates; collection is off in debug builds. Play's Data safety and the privacy policy declare them as crash logs and diagnostics.
- **Behind a contract**: `CrashReportingService` (`lib/data/services/crash_reporting/`) has a Crashlytics implementation and a no-op one for web (the plugin has no web support) and tests; `createCrashReportingService()` picks one. Plugins stay under `lib/data/`.
- **One installer per engine**: error handlers are per isolate, and the tracking service runs in its own `FlutterEngine`. `installCrashReporting(service, isolate: …)` starts the service, sets the `isolate` custom key (`ui` or `tracking_service`) and installs `FlutterError.onError` and `PlatformDispatcher.instance.onError` as fatal; `main()` and the tracking service's `onStart` each call it, the latter creating its own service.
- **Failures the app survives are non-fatal records** (`recordError(fatal: false)`), never `debugPrint` alone: the tracking service's plan read, its listeners, its publishing and its position stream. Expected refusals (`ServerRefusal`, a permission refusal, a GPS timeout, a disabled location service) are not recorded, and no recorded message may hold an e-mail, name or coordinates.
- **Android build**: the Crashlytics Gradle plugin uploads the R8 mapping, and `firebase-crashlytics-ndk` with `nativeSymbolUploadEnabled` in the release build type captures native crashes (`libflutter.so`, plugins) with symbolicated frames. Dart is not obfuscated, so Dart stack traces stay readable.
- **Known SDK limit**: right after a fresh install the SDK has no cached settings and queues fatal reports behind their download, so a second fatal error of the first launch can be lost if the process ends first (#250).
- The class diagram is in `docs/crash-reporting/class-diagram.md`.


## Testing

- Tests follow the versioned skills in `.agents/skills/`: `dart-add-unit-test`, `dart-generate-test-mocks`, `flutter-add-widget-test` and `flutter-add-integration-test` (and `dart-collect-coverage` for coverage).
- **Two kinds of test double**: services, use cases and view models are mockito mocks (`@GenerateNiceMocks`, the generated `*.mocks.dart` committed), and the unit tests of view models, use cases and repositories use them to verify interactions. Repositories in widget and router tests are the in-memory fakes of `testing/fakes/repositories/` (one `Fake<Name>` per repository of `lib/data/repositories/`, as in the Compass app): real behavior over in-memory data (creating a group lists it), failures a test asks for (`fail('create', const Failure.connection(FailedAction.create))` returns an `Err` with that `Failure`, or throws a `FakeFailure` carrying it for the repositories that throw), pending answers (`hold('create')` returns a `Completer`) and recorded calls (`callCount`, `argumentsOf`). Tests import them by relative path (`../../testing/fakes/...`); `flutter analyze` covers `testing/`, which holds no generated code. `test/testing/` tests the fakes that have logic.
- **The real router**: `test/support/test_app.dart` (`TestApp`) opens `buildAppRouter` with the real use cases over the fakes and mocks of the services, under the same types `appDependencies` provides; `test/routing/session_flow_test.dart` walks the session flow with it, and `test/config/dependencies_test.dart` checks that `appDependencies` provides every type `TestApp` does.
- The tests of a task are written by an agent with a different context window than the agent that did the main task.

Security rules are tested for real against the emulators in `test/rules/` (Node, `@firebase/rules-unit-testing`): `cd test/rules && npm ci && nice -n 10 npm test`. Each test file uses its own `demo-*` project id so they share one emulator run.

## Resource Limits on Dev Machines

Dev laptops have 8 GB of RAM shared with the IDE and a browser. Run heavy commands at low priority and low parallelism: `nice -n 15 flutter test -j 2`, `nice -n 15 dart run build_runner build` (never its `watch` mode), and Gradle with at most two workers (`org.gradle.workers.max=2` in `~/.gradle/gradle.properties`; `flutter build` gives no flag for it). The project caps the Gradle heap at 2 GB. Avoid running more than one build or test process at a time.


## Running the Web App Locally

Follow `docs/en-us/run-web-locally.md`. Serve `build/web` on `http://localhost:5000` with `dart run tool/serve_web.dart`: it is the only local origin the OAuth web client accepts for Google sign-in, and the app uses path URLs (`usePathUrlStrategy`, no `#`), so the server answers every path that is not a file with `index.html`. For unattended checks, capture the screenshot through CDP after Flutter paints, never with `--screenshot`.


## Git Workflow

- **Keep local `main` in sync**: Local `main` is a mirror of `origin/main` and must never receive direct commits. Before starting a task, fast-forward it: `git fetch origin main:main` from any other branch, or `git pull --ff-only` when `main` is checked out. If the fast-forward fails, local `main` has diverged; stop and report it instead of merging or resetting.
- **Check the remote before stating what was merged or released**: before saying a branch, PR or release is (or is not) on `main`, run `git fetch origin --tags` and check `origin/main`, the PR on GitHub and the tags. The local checkout, the session's start snapshot and agent notes are not evidence.
- **One branch per task**: Whenever a new task is assigned, create its issue first (see *Tracking Work in Issues*), then a new branch from the freshly synced `main` before making any changes (`git switch -c <type>/<short-description> main`). Never work directly on `main` or reuse a branch from a previous task.
- **Branch naming**: Prefix the branch with the change type, matching the commit convention (e.g. `feat/`, `fix/`, `chore/`, `docs/`, `build/`).
- **Pull request after human approval**: When the task is finished, wait for explicit human approval before opening a pull request to merge the branch into `main`. Do not open the PR, or merge it, without that approval. The PR description closes the task's issue (`Closes #<n>`).
- **GitHub interactions via MCP**: Interact with GitHub (pull requests, issues, reviews, projects, comments, etc.) preferably through the GitHub MCP server tools. Fall back to the `gh` CLI only when the GitHub MCP server is unavailable in the session or does not support the required operation.

## Releases

Every merge to `main` that touches the app is a release on the Play internal track; a human promotes chosen ones to closed testing with `promote-play-closed.yml` (walkthrough: `docs/en-us/publish-internal-testing.md`, section D).
- **versionName**: computed by the deploy from the Conventional Commits that touch the app since the last `v*` tag (`.github/scripts/next-version.sh`): `feat:` bumps the minor, `fix:` the patch; the deploy then tags the commit. Never edit `version:` in `pubspec.yaml`. **Never** use `!` or a `BREAKING CHANGE:` footer; the major version changes only when the human asks, through a commit with the footer `Release-As: X.0.0`.
- **Release notes**: every PR with a change visible to people using the app (`feat:` or `fix:` touching `lib/`, `android/` or `assets/`) rewrites `distribution/whatsnew/whatsnew-pt-BR`, `whatsnew-en-US`, `whatsnew-es-419` and `whatsnew-fr-FR`, the same content in the four languages (the file name is the Play language code, `es-419` for neutral Spanish and `fr-FR` for French; nothing else may live in that directory):
    - they cover **everything** visible since the `play-closed` tag, the last version promoted to closed testing (`git log play-closed..HEAD`; with no such tag, the whole app), because closed testers may skip versions; merge the new change into what is already there and drop what `play-closed` already contains;
    - write for end users: no technical terms, commit types, screen code names or issue numbers; at most 500 characters per file (`wc -m`), Play's limit; when space runs out, keep the most important changes;
    - the deploy fails before uploading if the files are missing, too long, or not updated since the last `v*` tag while the app changed (`.github/scripts/check-release-notes.sh`).

## Tracking Work in Issues

- **One issue per request, before starting**: Whenever the human asks for anything that changes the repository (code, tests, docs, configuration, rule files, this file), create its GitHub issue **before** making any change, then do the work. Two exceptions, where no new issue is created: a follow-up of a task that already has an issue (adjusting its open PR, answering review comments, fixing its CI) uses that issue; and a request already covered by an open issue reuses it (add the request as a comment). Requests that change nothing in the repository (questions, investigations, running the app) need no issue; whatever they find follows the next rule.
- **One issue per finding**: Whenever you find something that needs fixing (a bug, a regression, a misconfiguration, a security or data risk, an outdated doc), create a GitHub issue for it, unless the current task already fixes it. This includes findings outside the task's scope and problems in third-party code that affect this app; out-of-scope findings are not fixed in the current branch.
- **Before creating**: search the open issues to avoid duplicates; if one exists, add the request or what you found as a comment instead.
- **Content**: write it in English. For a finding, say what is wrong, how it was observed or reproduced, and its impact; for a request, say what was asked and why, with any decisions agreed with the human. Always end with a "Done when" checklist and link related issues and PRs.
- **Tracking**: add the issue to an organization project with Status `Todo`, and list the issues you created in the PR description and in your report to the human:
    - **"MonAmie Docs" (#3)** for documentation issues: those whose fix changes only documentation (`docs/`, READMEs, `AGENTS.md`, rule files);
    - **"MonAmie App" (#2)** for everything else, including issues whose fix changes both code and its documentation.

## Communicating with the Human

Every output to the human must be didactic: the goal is that they understand what happened and why, not only that the task is done.
- **Start from what they know**: define each technical term the first time it appears and do not assume prior knowledge of CI, Firebase, IAM, isolates or the plugins' internals.
- **Explain the why**: say the cause behind a result or a decision, pointing to the file and line that show it.
- **One analogy at most**, and only when it helps; replace it with another if the human does not understand it.
- **Show, do not only tell**: when a flow or lifecycle is hard to follow, offer a diagram in a standard notation (e.g. UML) instead of an invented one.
- **Be honest about limits**: say what was not checked or could not be verified.

## Language Conventions

- Commit messages must be written in English (subject and body), regardless of the language used in the conversation.
- Code comments (including doc comments `///`) must be written in English, regardless of the language used in the conversation.
- Exception: documentation files inside `docs/pt-br/` must be written in Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR).
- **Text people read lives in the ARB files**, never in the widgets: `lib/l10n/app_pt.arb` is the template (Brazilian Portuguese, every key with its `@key` entry and a `description`) and `lib/l10n/app_en.arb` has every key in English and `lib/l10n/app_es.arb` in neutral Spanish (*tú*) and `lib/l10n/app_fr.arb` in French (*vous*, with no-break spaces before `: ; ! ?` and inside « »), natural for end users (no `@key` entries: the template declares them). Screens read them with `AppLocalizations.of(context)`; text built from values uses placeholders, ICU `plural`/`select` and `DateTime` placeholders with an `intl` format (never string concatenation or a hand-made month list); every `plural` spells out `=0{…}`, because Portuguese's `one` case also covers 0 and `intl` would otherwise print the singular for zero, and the domain's values are named by `DomainLabels` (`lib/ui/core/ui/domain_labels.dart`). The app shows the language the person chose (`LanguageRepository`, kept on the device in `SharedPreferences`, empty until they choose) and otherwise the device's, English when the app does not have it (`AppLanguage.resolve`, `lib/ui/core/app_locales.dart`, English first in `appSupportedLocales`); Material's own texts (date picker, buttons) come from `flutter_localizations`.
- **To add a string**: add the key to `app_pt.arb` (with `@key.description` and, for placeholders, their `type` and `example`) and to `app_en.arb`, run `flutter gen-l10n` (or `flutter pub get`), and commit the regenerated `lib/l10n/app_localizations*.dart` with the ARB files (`l10n.yaml`; the PR checks regenerate them and fail on a difference, and fail too when an ARB file lacks a key of the template: `untranslated-messages-file`, `build/untranslated_messages.json`, must be `{}`). Brand names (MonAmie, Google, Google Meet, OpenStreetMap) may stay literal. `dart run tool/check_layers.dart` reports any other string literal with a letter in a hand-written file under `lib/ui/`, view models and states included (rule `ui_hard_coded_text`, `tool/src/ui_text_literals.dart`); a literal that is not text for people and fits none of its contexts takes `// check_layers: not_ui_text` at the end of its line. No layer writes a message for people outside the ARB files: failures and notices are typed (see *View Models & State*), and text needed where there is no `BuildContext` is handed in by the screen (the tracking service's notification, a `SharingNotification` the sharing switch builds from `AppLocalizations`; the texts shown when sharing resumes after a restart, a `SharingResumeTexts` stored by `SharingNotificationRefresher` for the service and the native receiver). Two exceptions, both data rather than UI text: the Cloud Functions' own explanations (`ServerRefusal`, pt-BR) and `UserModel.noName` ("Nome não informado"), which the tracking service publishes as the position's name and other devices read.
